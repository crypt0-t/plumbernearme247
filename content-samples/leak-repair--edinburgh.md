## Leak Detection & Repair in Edinburgh

Edinburgh's housing stock creates some genuinely tricky leak scenarios. Georgian flats in the New Town share plumbing stacks between multiple properties, meaning a leak on one floor often shows up as damp on another. In the Old Town, tenement pipework can be well over a hundred years old, with lead and iron pipes still in service beneath original floorboards. Newer developments in Leith and Granton bring their own complications — concealed pipework behind plasterboard and underfloor heating systems that aren't always straightforward to access.

---

## Why Edinburgh Properties Need Expert Leak Detection & Repair

The challenge with Edinburgh's older housing is that the buildings were never designed with modern plumbing in mind. Georgian and Victorian properties typically have thick stone walls and suspended timber floors, which can hide a slow leak for months before any visible damage appears. By that point, the moisture has often worked its way through joists and plaster, making the repair considerably more involved.

In tenement buildings specifically, shared drainage stacks mean that a blockage or joint failure in one flat can cause water ingress several floors down. Identifying who owns the problem — and which part of the stack is affected — requires careful investigation rather than guesswork.

Water pressure in Edinburgh can vary noticeably depending on whether a property is on a higher floor or in an elevated area like Morningside or Corstorphine. High pressure accelerates wear on older compression fittings and flexible hoses, which are common failure points in properties that haven't had recent plumbing upgrades.

Historic Edinburgh properties may also fall under conservation area restrictions, which affects how and where pipework can be rerouted. Any work on listed buildings requires particular care to avoid damage to original features, and some repairs need to be approved before work begins.

## What's Involved

The first step is locating the leak accurately. For visible leaks — a dripping joint under a sink or a weeping radiator valve — this is straightforward. For concealed leaks, a plumber will typically use acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging, or tracer gas to pinpoint the source without unnecessary damage to floors or walls.

Once located, the approach depends on what's been found. A failed compression fitting or a split flexible hose can usually be replaced within an hour. A corroded section of copper pipework behind a wall takes longer, particularly in a tenement where access may be restricted or the pipework serves multiple flats.

Most Edinburgh plumbers can attend within a few hours for urgent leaks, and same-day attendance is common for active water loss. For slower leaks that aren't causing immediate damage, a booked appointment within 24 to 48 hours is typical.

After the repair, it's worth asking the plumber to check surrounding pipework for signs of similar wear — particularly in older properties where one failing joint often indicates others nearby are in a similar condition.

## Leak Detection & Repair Costs in Edinburgh

For most straightforward repairs — replacing a joint, fitting a new section of copper pipe, or fixing a leaking valve — costs in Edinburgh typically fall between £100 and £250. More complex jobs involving concealed pipework, specialist detection equipment, or properties with difficult access sit in the £250 to £500 range.

Edinburgh-specific factors that affect cost include parking restrictions in the New Town and Old Town, which can add time and cost to any call-out. Tenement properties with no lift access mean more time carrying equipment. In conservation areas or listed buildings, certain repairs require additional care and documentation. Detection-only visits, where no repair is carried out on the day, are usually charged separately at around £80 to £150.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: My upstairs neighbour says they don't have a leak, but I've got a damp patch on my ceiling. What's going on?

This is a common situation in Edinburgh tenements. The water may not be coming from directly above — it can travel along joists or through shared pipework before appearing somewhere unexpected. A plumber can use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace the actual source, which sometimes turns out to be a slow drip from a stack pipe rather than anything in the flat above.

### Q: I've got a Georgian flat in the New Town with original floorboards. Can a leak be found without lifting them?

In many cases, yes. Acoustic detection and thermal imaging can identify moisture and active leaks through floorboards without needing to lift them immediately. That said, if the leak is confirmed beneath the floor, some access will eventually be needed — but a good plumber will keep this to a minimum and work with the existing boards where possible.

### Q: The water meter is running when everything's switched off. How serious is this?

It means water is actively escaping somewhere in your system, even if you can't see or hear it. Underground supply pipes and concealed runs within walls are the most common culprits. Left unaddressed, this will cause structural damage over time and add significantly to your water bill. It's worth getting it investigated promptly rather than waiting to see if it resolves itself — it won't.

### Q: We're in a newer build in Leith with underfloor heating. Can leaks in that system be detected without digging up the screed?

Yes, tracer gas detection is the standard method for underfloor pipe leaks and doesn't require breaking into the floor to locate the problem first. Once the leak is pinpointed, only a small section needs to be accessed for the repair, rather than lifting the entire floor to investigate.
